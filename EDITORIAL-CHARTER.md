# FOOTNOTE Editorial Charter

## What FOOTNOTE is

FOOTNOTE is an independent magazine and cultural archive documenting Jammu and Kashmir's voices, music, languages, creative scenes and stories. Hip-hop sits at the centre of its contemporary cultural coverage.

It is not only a hip-hop page, a video channel or a generic regional news outlet. It is a considered cultural publication with room for reporting, interviews, essays, criticism, photography, archival research and oral history.

## Editorial scope

FOOTNOTE may cover:

- Hip-hop, independent music and the people who build local scenes.
- Folk music, performance and musical traditions.
- Regional languages, poetry, literature and storytelling.
- Visual art, photography, design and other creative practices.
- Cultural memory, archives, oral histories and local histories.
- The relationship between place, identity, sound and community.

Its regional lens includes Jammu, Poonch, Rajouri, the Chenab Valley and the Kashmir Valley. Coverage should not treat the region as culturally uniform.

## Audience

The magazine speaks to young readers following music, art, language and culture, as well as older readers interested in preserving memory, history and local knowledge.

## Editorial principles

1. **Accuracy:** Verify factual claims before publication.
2. **Attribution:** Make clear where information comes from.
3. **Primary evidence:** Prefer interviews, original documents, archival records and first-hand accounts.
4. **Context:** Do not reduce a person, community, tradition or scene to a headline.
5. **Fairness:** Represent sources faithfully and give subjects a fair opportunity to respond where appropriate.
6. **Independence:** Clearly disclose sponsorships, conflicts of interest and paid placements.
7. **Rights:** Secure permission or confirm lawful use before publishing photographs, recordings, artwork or excerpts.
8. **Corrections:** Correct material errors transparently.
9. **Respect:** Handle language, cultural practices, memory and identity with care.

## Research and citations

Use numbered references in the relevant pages or stories when the format calls for them. Include complete citations in the final references section of an issue. Interviews should be identified as interviews, with dates and relevant context. Do not present unverified recollections as established historical fact.

## Visual direction

The visual language should feel literary, archival and documentary rather than templated. The initial references discussed for the magazine include The FADER, The Wire and The Face. FOOTNOTE should develop its own visual identity using confident typography, warm paper tones, black ink, deep red accents and authentic documentary imagery.

Do not use fabricated archival photographs, generic AI portraits or synthetic imagery presented as documentary evidence.

## What does not count as finished editorial work

A topic idea is not a reported story. A generated script is not fact-checked journalism. A design mock-up is not a completed issue. Publication requires editing, verification, rights checks and final proofing.
