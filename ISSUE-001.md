# Issue 001 — The Sound We Made

**Publication:** FOOTNOTE  
**Issue:** 001  
**Working theme:** The Sound We Made

## Editorial premise

The first issue looks at how sound, music and memory shape cultural identity across Jammu and Kashmir. Contemporary hip-hop is a central part of the issue, alongside distinct coverage of older musical traditions, regional languages and cultural histories.

The issue should document people and scenes with care. It should not assume that one city, language, genre or community represents the entire region.

## Regional scope

The planned regional lens includes:

- Jammu
- Poonch
- Rajouri
- Chenab Valley
- Kashmir Valley

## Planned editorial directions

These are starting points for reporting and commissioning. They are not completed or verified stories.

### 1. Before the Spotlight: The People Who Built Jammu Hip-Hop

A feature focused on the people, collectives, venues, producers, performers and informal networks that helped build the Jammu hip-hop scene. Establish a clear timeline through interviews and verifiable evidence.

### 2. The Sound We Made

The issue's central exploration of contemporary music and cultural expression in Jammu and Kashmir. Include first-hand voices and explain the local context behind the work.

### 3. Language, poetry and place

Explore how regional languages and forms of poetry carry memory, identity and creative expression. Commission voices from different parts of the region rather than treating them as interchangeable.

### 4. Older musical traditions

Give folk and traditional music its own editorial space. One proposed direction is a feature on Romalo Ram and Dogri folk music, subject to research, sourcing and editorial verification.

### 5. A cultural map of the region

Build a set of stories that reflect the distinct cultural contexts of Jammu, Poonch, Rajouri, Chenab Valley and Kashmir Valley.

## Research requirements

Before a story is approved for publication:

- Confirm names, spellings, dates, locations and chronology.
- Seek direct interviews and primary sources wherever possible.
- Verify archival material with a traceable institution, collection or record.
- Record source details and permissions.
- Use numbered references on pages where appropriate.
- Place complete citations in the issue's final references section.
- Obtain image, artwork and recording permissions.
- Separate verified facts from personal recollection, interpretation and opinion.

## Production direction

The first issue was discussed as a 14-page magazine concept. The intended feel is retro-literary, documentary and archival, with strong typography, warm paper tones, black ink and deep red accents. The cover concept previously explored was **Before the Beat, There Was Rhythm**, using a clearly sourced archival image or a labelled placeholder until an authentic image is secured.

The final page should include full citations for numbered references used throughout the issue.
